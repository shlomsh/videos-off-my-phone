# Name and message

Chosen by the user on 26 Nov 2026: **Videos Off My Phone**. Repo: `videos-off-my-phone`.

## The point of the tool
Your Google storage (15 GB free) is shared by Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos. **YouTube is not part of it.** Google's list of what uses storage has no YouTube, and its storage policy names YouTube among the services it does not apply to. So a video moved to your own YouTube channel stops using your Google storage, and stops using space on your phone once Google Photos syncs the delete.

Most people don't know this. It is the first thing the app and its listing should say.

Two more things to say every time: **free** and **open source**.

## Draft listing text
- **Store title:** Videos Off My Phone – Free Up Storage for Google Photos™
- **Short description** (store limit 132 characters): Free and open source. Move big videos to your own YouTube channel, which doesn't use your Google storage, then delete the originals.
- **Opening lines (README and store):**
  - Your phone and your Google storage fill up with videos. YouTube storage doesn't count toward your Google storage.
  - Videos Off My Phone moves your biggest videos from Google Photos to your own YouTube channel, in full original quality. You choose who can watch: only you, people with the link, or everyone.
  - It checks that the copy is complete, then deletes the Google Photos original for you, one confirmed video at a time. Google Photos then removes it from your phone.
  - Free and open source. No ads, no account with us, no server of ours. Your videos go straight from Google Photos to YouTube.
- **Attribution line:** Google Photos and YouTube are trademarks of Google LLC. This app is not made or endorsed by Google.

## Words to avoid
- "YouTube" or "YT" in the name, the icon or the domain (YouTube API branding rules).
- "Unlimited" or "free storage forever": YouTube can change its rules, and there are upload limits.
- Saying the space is free the moment a video is deleted. Google Photos keeps the trash for 30 days, and Google doesn't say whether the trash still counts.
